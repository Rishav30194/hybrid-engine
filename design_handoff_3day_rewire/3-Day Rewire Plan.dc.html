Program plan Hybrid Engine · v2

Three days. Forty-five minutes.
Same two goals.

The current program spends 5 sessions and roughly 85 hard sets a week. The rewire spends 3 sessions and about 38. This document says what that costs, what it protects, and how the week is laid out — before any code changes. Locked to Mon / Wed / Fri, restarting at Week 1, with Monday carrying the extra ten minutes when you have them.

01 · The verdict

Strength is cheaper to keep than it looks. Endurance is the half that actually gets squeezed — and the squeeze happens in the easy aerobic work, not the hard stuff.

Protected
Squat, bench/OHP and trap-bar deadlift keep progressing. Each still gets 4 hard sets a week at 70–85% — above the 2–3 sets per exercise that the minimum-dose literature says drives 1RM.
Protected
VO₂max. Interval work returns roughly twice the aerobic gain per minute of moderate steady work, so the ceiling survives inside a 15-minute block.
At risk
Aerobic base and arm/shoulder size. Zone 2 shrinks to a single 15-minute tail on Friday plus your daily walking, and the Day 5 pump session disappears entirely.
02 · What you lose
1
Zone 2 volume — the real casualty
Days 3 and 5 currently carry your easy aerobic work. Nothing in a 45-minute strength session can replace it. Left unreplaced, you keep a high VO₂max sitting on a thinner base: interval sessions feel harder, recovery between them slows.
Mitigation — a fixed 15-minute Zone 2 tail on Friday, every week, plus your daily walking. It is less base than five days bought you, but it is scheduled rather than hoped for — which is the difference that matters.
2
Hypertrophy rate on the small stuff
Day 5 was 24 sets of arms, delts, upper back and calves. Direct arm work drops to about 2 sets a week and calves to 2. Those muscles move from growing to holding. Chest, back, quads and hams stay above the growth threshold.
Mitigation — the arm and lateral sets sit inside rest periods, so they cost no extra minutes. Accept slower arm growth; it's the cheapest thing to give up.
3
Sled and carry volume
Three sled slots become one. Sled push is the best minute-for-minute item you own — hard conditioning with almost no eccentric damage — so it keeps its place, but sled drag and the max-distance carry work come out.
4
Overhead press as its own lift
It stays as bench's every-third-week alternate, which means it progresses at roughly a third the rate. Pick one to be the number you care about; the other rides along.
5
Margin for a missed session
One skipped day used to cost 20% of the week. Now it costs 33%, and it removes a main lift entirely. The plan needs a stated rule for this, not improvisation — see §06.
03 · What the evidence supports
Upper- and lower-body 1RM keep climbing on 2–3 hard sets per exercise per week at 70–85% taken close to failure. Hypertrophy's floor is about 4 sets per muscle group per week at 6–15 reps.
Androulakis-Korakakis meta-analysis; Iversen review — summarised in ACSM's Health & Fitness Journal, Jan 2026
Trained lifters hold strength and size on roughly a third of their usual volume for months — as long as the load on the bar stays high. Cut volume, never intensity.
Bickel et al.; Spiering et al. 2021, minimal-dose review
The interference effect is much weaker than the 1980 Hickson result implied. A 2025 trial of 96 people found concurrent aerobic and strength training impaired neither maximal strength, explosive strength, nor hypertrophy — whether or not the cardio hit the same muscles as the lifting.
Feuerbacher et al., Scand J Med Sci Sports, Feb 2025
Order and same-day scheduling are not the main driver of interference. Across 43 studies, sequence and session arrangement didn't blunt hypertrophy or maximal strength. Lift first anyway — it protects the quality of the sets that matter.
Schumann et al. meta-analysis; Frontiers in Sports & Active Living, Dec 2025
Interval work produces roughly double the VO₂max gain of moderate continuous training, and 3–5 minute efforts produce the largest jumps. This is what makes a 15-minute engine block viable.
Bacon 2013; Milanović 2015; Weston 2014; Helgerud 2007 (4×4)
With weekly volume held equal, frequency has little independent effect. Three full-body days can carry what five split days carried, minus the volume you genuinely cut.
Pelland et al., dose-response meta-regressions, 2025

Two caveats worth holding: most of these trials run 8–12 weeks with fewer than 30 people per group, and "no interference detected" is not the same as "no interference at elite margins." At your volume it is a safe bet.

06 · Rules that make it hold
→
Cut volume, never intensity. The whole plan rests on loads staying at 70–85% of 1RM. Dropping the weight to "make it easier" is what causes detraining, not the missing sets.
→
Lift first, always. Sequence isn't the main interference driver, but the main lift gets your freshest nervous system regardless.
→
Mon / Wed / Fri, fixed. One rest day between sessions, two before Monday. Never two of the three back to back.
→
Missed a day? Skip it, don't stack it. Slide the week's remaining sessions along and carry the missed anchor into next week's slot — the app can rotate it automatically rather than doubling a session.
→
Progress one dial at a time. Strength: bump the 1RM when the top set beats its target RPE. Conditioning: more rounds or longer work or heavier sled — never two at once.
→
Under 40 minutes? Cut in this order: B-pair accessories → A-pair third round → sled or interval rounds. The anchor lift and Friday's Zone 2 tail never get cut.
→
Extra time goes to Monday. Only Monday has an extension block. Wednesday and Friday stay at 45 even on a good week — spare minutes there are better spent leaving.

08 · What this means for the app
Unchanged: the 1RM inputs, the %1RM ladder across weeks 1–8, load recalculation, rest timer, check-off state, the three-tab shell.
Changes: five days become three, named Mon / Wed / Fri; conditioning keys drop from five to three (sled, intervals, Zone 2); the block restarts at Week 1 on your existing 1RMs.
New: a per-session time budget, an optional extension block that only Monday shows, a daily-walk check-off, and a rotation rule for a missed session.
All of it fits the existing data model — this is a content rewrite plus two new fields, not a rebuild.
